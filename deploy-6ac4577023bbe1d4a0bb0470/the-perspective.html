FYPM Perspective · 6 Oct 2026

A verdict before the deck.

Most investors who look at Indonesia already know the headline story: a large, young population, a growing digital economy, deep natural resources. What they rarely get, early enough, is a straight answer to the question that actually matters — does this specific deal work here, and on what terms?

The traditional route to that answer is slow. A mandate is scoped, a team is assembled, and a long report arrives weeks later. By then the seller has moved, the valuation has moved, or the regulation has.

Answer first

FYPM Insight reverses the order. You describe the deal; the engine returns a verdict — Go, Conditional Go or Hold — on the first page, weighted to the posture you set. Everything after that is the working: eight dimensions, each claim tagged by source and dated.

Honest about what it doesn't know

An AI engine reading regulations and reporting will always meet questions it can't settle from a desk: a grid connection date, how a regional office is actually applying a rule, whether a counterparty is who they say they are. Insight doesn't guess. It flags those items as needing a field check.

Then people on the ground

That is where the firm comes in. Flagged items escalate to our team in Indonesia — the same senior people who have spent more than a decade advising investors in-country. They verify, and the verdict comes back marked FYPM verified.

Speed from the desk, certainty from the ground. One pipeline.
FYPM Perspective · 6 Oct 2026

Eight questions every Indonesia deal has to answer.

Deals in Indonesia rarely fail on the investment thesis. They fail on something specific and knowable that nobody checked in time. These are the eight questions we put to every deal.

1. Can you own it the way you want to?

Foreign-ownership limits and the investment list decide the vehicle. Get this wrong and everything downstream is built on sand.

2. Which permits, from whom, and when?

The licence on paper and the licence in practice are not always the same timeline.

3. Is the power — and the road — really there?

Grid capacity and connection dates are where many infrastructure plans quietly slip.

4. What do sector and data rules require?

Data residency, local content and sector-specific regulation can reshape the operating model.

5. What is the real tax position?

Incentives exist; qualifying for them, and keeping them, is the work.

6. Can you get money in and out?

Repatriation, forex and hedging costs belong in the model from day one.

7. Can you hire the people you need?

Expatriate permits and local talent depth set the pace of any ramp-up.

8. What could change around you?

Policy direction, election cycles and external shocks — from tariffs to energy prices.

FYPM Insight scores each of the eight, tags every claim by source, and flags what needs someone on the ground to confirm.
